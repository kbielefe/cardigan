# Domain

Knitting on a Silver Reed LK150 or a Taitexma TH-160 with a TR-160 ribber, and the way written patterns talk about that work.

Pitch is the spacing of the needles on the bed. Fabric gauge comes from a swatch.

| Machine | Needles | Pitch | 100 stitches of bed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Silver Reed LK150 | 150 | 6.5 mm | 65 cm (650 mm) |
| Taitexma TH-160 | 164 | 6.0 mm | 60 cm (600 mm) |

Needle-bed width is stitch count times pitch. Finished width comes from the confirmed swatch. The same 100 stitches at 20 stitches per 10 cm finish at 50 cm on either machine. The beds under them are 650 mm and 600 mm. A centimeter measurement written for one machine is a measurement, and a tension-dial number belongs to the dial it was written for.

A count past 150 does not fit the LK150. A count past 164 does not fit the TH-160.

A step can be knitted on either machine when both machines have that feature. The brand and the lever names are the wording. The action is what the carriage does. A ribber, the AG11, thread lace, and weaving exist on one of these machines.

## LK150

The LK150 has one bed, 150 needles, and the AG11 intarsia carriage. It has no ribber, no second bed, and no racking.

Needle positions, back to front, are A, B, C, D. Back is out of work.

- A is out of the run. The carriage ignores it.
- B knits in plain knitting. The side lever on the circle slips it.
- C knits stockinette. The AG11 wants needles here with the latches open, so yarn can be laid in the hooks.
- D is holding. It stays in the fabric. Russel lever 1 leaves it. Russel lever 2 knits it back to B. Tuck leaves needles in D for a few passes, then knits them back.

The side levers control B. The triangle, also written `^` or "back", knits B. The circle, also written O, dot, spot, or "forward", slips B. The Russel levers, also spelled Russell, control D. I, also 1, one, or "back", holds D. II, also 2, two, or "forward", knits D back to B. Plain knitting is the triangle and 2.

Only the lever on the end the carriage is leaving acts on that pass. Patterns set both sides, set the left and the right differently, or name only one side. When the two ends are set differently and the trip names no end, the direction of that pass is not determined.

Feeder 1 is the front slot and the main yarn. Feeder 2 is plating, a second yarn knitted at the same time. Single-colour garment patterns do not name a feeder. Fairisle text says "the feeder", yarn rests, and MC and CC. On the FC6 fairisle carriage the main-carriage slots are reversed, and feeder 2 knits the D needles. The AG11 has no feeder.

The needle butts are banded in groups of 10. The bed ruler is numbered from a 0 in the middle, 75 needles each side, with a mark every 10.

The LK150 does plain, tuck, and slip by cam, fair isle by selecting needles, and plating. Intarsia is the AG11, a different carriage from the TH-160's built-in intarsia. Thread lace, weaving, and a ribber bed are TH-160 work.

## TH-160 and TR-160

The TH-160 has 164 needles. The TR-160 is its ribber, another 164 needles, and it sits in front of the main bed.

Needle positions are A, B, D, and E. There is no C.

- A is out of the run.
- B is plain knitting. Cast-on brings needles to B. In the automatic cast-on, B is the needles that slip.
- D is the upper position, selected for hand patterning. It is the place where yarn is laid, and it is holding on the LK150.
- E is holding. Cam levers on H leave it. N knits it. Tuck brings pattern needles to E. The automatic cast-on uses E for the one needle that wraps.

"Pull to D" is a different needle on the TH-160 than on the LK150. The word "hold" is the holding role on either machine. The letter D, with no machine named and no word for a role, does not say which position it is. Needle letters follow the machine the pattern names.

Cam levers are left and right, marked N and H. N knits. H holds the E needles, and in the tuck chart "Cam Lever L/R to H" lays the yarn on the E needles. A pass with "Cam Lever R to N" knits those needles. The same chart then changes only the right lever for the rest of the repeat. The change lever is a separate control, named in the parts list next to the tension dial and the cam levers. Its stops are Intarsia (1), Plain (P1), and Part (P2). The manual also writes those stops as INTAR and PART. The Brother KH230 book uses its change lever for plain, partial, and intarsia. That book matches this wording and is a Brother manual.

The parts list names two feeders: Yarn Feeder and Plating Yarn Feeder. Intarsia stays on this carriage. The slots are left empty and each color is laid in the hooks across its needles.

The TH-160 does built-in intarsia, fair isle, tuck, slip, thread lace, weaving, and motif.

The bed ruler is numbered from a 0 in the middle, 82 needles each side, with a mark every 10. A centered run of 40 is left 20 through right 20. The plain-knitting sample in the manual is 40 needles, 20 on each side of center 0. A written range such as 1–40 is a stitch count.

The stitch-size dial belongs to the yarn and rarely changes from pass to pass. The pass number is a counter, written in words. Written tension includes "tension 8", "carriage tension", "T.D. 6", "tighter 2 full numbers", and "reset carriage tension to stockinette gauge". Fairisle sheets go up one number from stockinette. The Brother KH230 plain page that matches the TH-160 paragraph uses tension dial 6.

## Shared needle roles

The roles are the same kind of work on both beds. The letter is the machine's name for that role.

| Role | LK150 | TH-160 | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Out of work | A | A | Ignored. |
| Working | B | B | Knits in plain knitting. Slips when the carriage is set to slip. |
| Upper | C | D | Latches open for laying yarn. On the TH-160 this is also the selected position for hand patterning. |
| Holding | D | E | Stays in the fabric. Tuck lays yarn over these needles. Short rows leave them until the carriage is set to knit them back. |

## Carriage direction

A carriage end is left, right, or either. A setup step has no end.

Direction is written when the next selection, the yarn change, or a one-sided shaping depends on it. Words in the patterns: COR, COL, "carriage on the left", "carriage on the right", "knit 1 row to the left", "move the carriage to the left", "move the carriage to the right", "pass the carriage right", "pass the carriage left", and chart arrows. Eileen Montgomery changes colours when the carriage is on the left and selects needles when it is on the right. Many plain rows name no direction. An unnamed end on a plain trip can be taken from either side. Left or right is not filled in when the pattern leaves it out.

## A pass and a row

A pass is one trip of the carriage, or a setup step that is not a trip. A row is whatever that pattern says it is. Row is not a stable count.

LK150 garments and the TH-160 numbered steps treat one pass as one row, and the row counter agrees. They say "Knit N rows", "Knit 1 row", and "K (6, 6, 6) rows". Each of those counts one carriage trip as one row. "Knit 54 rows" is 54 trips that share that sentence. A sentence that only sets the carriage is a setup step, and the trips after it carry the resulting cam. Several actions in one paragraph are several passes.

LK150 fairisle methods 1, 2, and 4 take two or more passes for one knitted row, and the row counter reads double. Method 3 and the FC6 are one pass per knitted row, and the counter agrees. When the pattern says two passes complete one knitted row, that knitted row is two trips. When it does not say, a garment row is one trip.

The row counter belongs to the section. "RC 0", "RC reset to 000", and "row counter set to 000" start that section's count again. The simple-stitch sweater resets it on the second side of the neck. A sleeve starts its own count. "Knit N rows" and "K N rows" are a count. "Knit to row N" and "Knit to RC N" are a counter target, and the trips exist only when the counter at that sentence is known. "RC N" after a block is a checkpoint.

## Shared actions

These are the actions the words collapse to. Each is portable across the LK150, the TH-160, and any other machine that has that feature.

- Knit a pass. "Knit N rows", "K N rows", "knit 1 row", "move the carriage", "pass the carriage".
- Carriage end. See above. Absent on plain stockinette.
- Select needles into a role. By count each side of 0, by needle number from 0, by "every Nth", by a chart read from the bottom, or by "the left side of the bed". "20-20", "needle #19", and "cast on 62, 31 each side" are the same kind of selection. A sentence that only pulls needles out is a setup, and it is not a trip.
- Plain. Working needles knit. LK150 side levers on the triangle. TH-160 plain setting. Brother plain. Garment patterns often name no lever.
- Slip. Working needles do not knit. Selected needles further forward do. LK150 side levers on the circle, with Russel II. TH-160 "cam to slip", with B slipping and E knitting. Fairisle method 1 is this action, one color per pass.
- Hold. Holding needles stay. "Russel I", "Cam Lever H", "part buttons", "set not to knit the needles in hold".
- Knit the holding needles back. "Russel II", "Cam Lever N", "set machine to knit all needles".
- Tuck. Hold for several passes so the yarn lays on those needles, then knit them back. "Yarn will layover needles in hold", "the yarn is laid on the needles".
- Intarsia. Lay each color in the hooks of its needles and pass with an empty feeder. The LK150 swaps to the AG11 and uses C. The TH-160 stays on the main carriage. The carriage swap and the change lever are setup for this action.
- Yarn in a feeder, or parked in a yarn rest. Feeder 1, feeder 2, "the feeder", Yarn Feeder, and Plating Yarn Feeder name slots. Which slot is main or plating is the machine's setup. An unnamed feeder on a single-colour trip names no slot. "MC in feeder" is the main slot. Feeder 2 or the plating feeder is plating.
- Shape. Increase, decrease, transfer onto the next needle, move a group and fill a needle, short-row wrap of the first unworked needle, bind off, cast off, scrap off. A hand transfer written as a rib is a shape step on either machine. A decrease on a trip is shape, with the cam still plain.
- Mark. A thread, a dry-erase mark, or "mark edge stitches". This is remembered, and it is not a carriage trip.
- Setup. A cam change, a row-counter reset, or another sentence that only sets the carriage.

This list covers the patterns below. It is not a dictionary of every lever name.

## How written patterns talk

A sweater says "Knit 54 rows". The TH-160 manual says "Set the Cam Lever L/R to H. Move the carriage to left." No published TH-160 garment in the sweater-prose style is in the sources.

Patterns this description follows:

- City Farmhouse Studio, Machine Knit Wrap Sweater Scarf (2025-01-23). Three sizes in parentheses. Tuck by selecting needles. 2×1 rib by hand transfer.
- City Farmhouse Studio, Machine Knit Simple Stitch Sweater (2025-09-26). Nine sizes, parentheses then square brackets. Short-row neck, decreases, garter by hand transfer.
- City Farmhouse Studio, Machine Knit Diamond Canvas Sweater (2025-12-13). Same size notation. Tuck panels. Decreases inside asterisks. The glossary defines COR and RC. The page is not in the saved sources.

LK150 stitch writing:

- LK150 instruction book, as walked by Irene Woods, Clearwater Knits, LK 150 Fairisle Tutorial. Chart, Russel levers, side levers, two passes for one knitted row. The saved book is a scan.
- Eileen Montgomery, LK150 Fairisle (1999, 2008). Four methods. COL, COR, feeder, yarn rest, MC and CC, ndls, side-lever marks, Russel I and II, the main carriage and the intarsia carriage.
- Creative Tien, Tuck stitch blanket on an LK150 (2022-05-05). Russell lever I and II, D position, every 5th needle, tension numbers.

TH-160 and TR-160:

- Taitexma TH-160 / 230 instruction manual. Parts list, plain knitting, intarsia, tuck, and one tuck chart: 1 pattern, 4 stitches, 8 rows.
- Taitexma TR-160/230 instruction manual. Parts list: cam lever L and R, tucking lever, tension dial, racking indicator, yarn rest. The stitch sections are 1×1 rib, 2×2 rib, full needle rib, 5×5 rib, circular knitting, English rib, and racking patterns. Fisherman rib is not in this text.
- Knit It Now, Taitexma 160 automatic cast-on (2023-05-15). Cam on slip, carriage on the left, one needle to E, pass right, pass left. Knit It Now names the TR-160 fabrics as 1×1 rib, full needle rib, English rib, fisherman rib, circular or tubular, and hold, plus racking.
- Brother KH230 plain knitting matches the TH-160 paragraph (40 needles, 20 each side of center 0, tension dial 6). Brother KR230 1×1 is the ribber wording that book uses. That lesson sets the main change lever to plain, holding cam levers to N N, the ribber tucking lever to R, and ribber cam levers to N N, then uses partial for the circular cast-on rows and plain again for the rib. It is a Brother lesson.

A pattern written for a bulky machine, for contrast with these two:

- Pat Tittizer, Wheelchair Blanket (2007-09-08). RC 0, pull out 20-20, part buttons, carriage on the left. It also says "needle #19 on each side" and "needles 21-24, 26, 28-32".

Other patterns for size and repeat wording: Summer in the City (2022) and Summer Breeze.

Abbreviation lists at Clearwater Knits and The Knit Tree both say machine-knitting abbreviations are not standard across publishers. The pattern's own glossary wins for that pattern.

### Needle numbers

Numbers on these beds start at a center 0. "20-20", "20 on either side", and "needle #19" are needle numbers. "Cast on 62" and "31 each side" are stitch counts. An odd count puts the extra needle on one side, and the side has to be named. A range such as 21-24 is needle numbers when the sentence is about the bed, and a stitch count when the sentence says stitches or needles in work. A bare range with neither word can be read either way. A written range such as 1–40 is a stitch count.

Verbs: "pull out", "bring forward", "push back", "place in hold", "move into working position". "Hang" means put stitches or a hem back on needles.

### Shaping

"Increase 1 stitch every N rows. Alternate side of bed of each increase." "Decrease one stitch on each side." Diamond Canvas decreases inside the asterisks: decrease one stitch, leave one needle with only one stitch, decrease the next stitch onto the next needle, then move all stitches in work together.

Short rows in the simple-stitch sweater: put a group in hold, set the carriage so it does not knit needles in hold, knit 1 row, wrap the yarn around the first unworked needle, knit 1 row. The second side says "move carriage to left side" and resets the row counter.

Edges: "mark edge stitches", "mark edges for sleeve location". Bind off is the garment word. The wheelchair blanket says "cast off" and "take off on waste yarn". Cast-on is "e-wrap", "ewrap", or "ewrap cast on", often with waste yarn and ravel cord.

Rib in the LK150 garments is a hand transfer: "hand transfer every 3rd stitch to a purl" or "hand transfer the second row stitches to be purl stitches", with the tension "tighter 2 full numbers". That is a hand transfer on either machine. A TR-160 rib is knitted on the ribber.

A number that schedules shaping on a row, and does not say whether the work happens before the carriage moves or when the counter already shows that number, does not fix the moment of the shaping.

### Sizes

The first number stands alone. The next sizes sit in parentheses. The largest sit in square brackets. An example is `62 (66, 72, 78, 86) [92, 100, 108, 118]`. The wrap scarf uses parentheses only, for three sizes. Summer in the City also puts bare numbers between the parentheses and the brackets: `90 (98, 106, 114) 122, 138 [146, 154, 170]`, matching `XS (S, M, L) XL, 2X [3X, 4X, 5X]`. A bare number with no group is the first size. Size words in that example are XS, S, M, L, XL, 2X, 3X, 4X, and 5X. The same notation also uses XXS, XXL, and XXXL.

A parenthesis full of words is a comment, as in `RC (Knit rows to underarm)` and `(make 2)`. A fraction stays a fraction. A line such as "For sizes XS to L" selects a block of sizes. The other size blocks stay in the pattern.

A number group whose count does not match the size line is a different kind of number from the sizes.

### Repeats

Asterisks: "Repeat from * to *" and the misspelling "Repeat form * to *". The total includes the first time. "N more times" is extra. The RC line after the repeat is the check, and it stays its own sentence. Also: "continue alternating for a total of N rows", "repeat for a total of N times worked", "every 4th needle", "every sixth needle", "every 5th needle", "1 x 3 needle pusher", and "1 pattern 4 stitch, 8 rows".

A repeat that does not close is an asterisk with no repeat sentence, a repeat sentence with no span, or nested asterisks. A phrase that is neither "for a total of N" nor "N more times" does not give a count. A repeat whose checkpoint disagrees with the rows inside it does not give a settled count.

### Abbreviations

Tokens whose meaning is taken from the glossary when the pattern defines them, and from the usual machine-knitting sense when it does not: R.C., T.D., COR, COL, MC, CC, RC, ndl or ndls, K before a number, CO, BO, MT, EON. Summer in the City's glossary defines COR, COL, MT, and K followed by a number. Diamond Canvas defines COR and RC as the row count.

These tokens have two published meanings:

- COL is carriage on the left, and some magazines use it for color.
- RC is the row counter, and it is also return cams.
- R.C. is ravel cord.
- CC is contrast color, and it is also clearing cams.

### Sections of a pattern

A garment opens with yarn, machine, gauge, and size, then a glossary and notes, then a heading per piece. Headings in the patterns include Body, Sleeve (make 2), Front, Back, Side Panels, and Construction. "Begin Neck Shaping" is a heading in the middle of Front. The row-counter checkpoint ends the piece. Construction, seaming, i-cord as a finishing note, "How to Wrap", and blocking sit after the last bind-off and are finishing, so they are not carriage trips. The manuals use numbered steps and a chart title ("2-1 Plain knitting", "1 pattern 4 stitch, 8 rows") in place of garment headings.

Glossary, notes, finishing, abbreviations, how to wrap, blocking, and materials are written around the knitting. The wheelchair blanket's Finishing is latching and seaming.

### Charts

A chart stays with its section. A fairisle chart is read from the bottom. Marked squares are the needles to pull. Eileen's studio method draws two chart lines for one knitted row. The manual method uses one line per pass.

When the section says which edge is read first, and whether one line is one trip or one knitted row, the trips follow that order. When the section does not say, the chart does not fix the needle order. Fairisle methods 1, 2, and 4 do say so.

A hyphen at the end of a line may be a word broken across the line. "e-wrap" and "2×1" contain real hyphens.

## Ambiguous wording

The action can be named and the pass still be unsettled:

- The work is scheduled on a row number and the pattern does not say whether it happens before the carriage moves.
- A number may be needle numbers from center 0 or a stitch count, and the sentence does not lean one way.
- An odd count names no side.
- The pattern does not say whether one knitted row is one trip or two.
- The two carriage ends are set differently and this trip has no end.
- The letter D, and the pattern names neither a machine nor a role.

"Knit to row N", when the counter at that sentence is not known, does not determine the trips in between.

## Techniques

A short two-color repeat is LK150 slip fair isle. A long float is TH-160 built-in intarsia. More than two colors and a long float is LK150 AG11 intarsia. A clean back is TH-160 double-bed jacquard, with the floats between the two beds. Each of these needs the machine that can make it.

A float is the gap between two stitches of the same color. Its length is the skipped stitches between them. The machine will knit a long float. The length is still worth knowing, with the row it sits on.

### Fair isle

LK150 fairisle methods 1, 2, and 4 take two or more passes for one knitted row, and the counter reads double. Method 3 and the FC6 are one pass per knitted row. Method 1 is slip, one color per pass. FC6 one-pass fairisle can be knitted as slip, one color per pass, on either machine. The chart is the pattern. The TH-160 lists fair isle as a built-in technique.

### Tuck and slip

Tuck and slip use the same kind of needle selection as fair isle, and a different cam. Tuck in prose is hold for several passes so the yarn lays over the selected needles, then knit them back. The TH-160 tuck chart numbers each pass: move the carriage to the left, then to the right, as its own step.

### Intarsia

A row of color blocks. Each block is a span of one yarn, laid in the hooks. A gap inside a block is not a float, and a color that returns after a gap is not a float. LK150 intarsia uses the AG11. TH-160 intarsia uses the built-in carriage.

### Double-bed jacquard

One design row on the TH-160 becomes face passes and ribber backing passes. The face passes are slip. The backing is named. The LK150 has no ribber for this separation. One design row is several carriage trips.

### Thread lace, weaving, and motif

Thread lace and weaving are on the TH-160. Motif is listed on the TH-160, and no pass rules for motif are written here.

### Mechanisms with no needle chart

A punchcard, a lace carriage, a garter carriage, or a colour changer with no chart of selected needles does not say which needles work. A chart of selected needles is the action. The card or the carriage that would have selected them is the mechanism. A lace carriage is outside both of these machines. Thread lace and weaving are TH-160 techniques. The AG11 is the LK150's intarsia carriage.

A hand transfer written as a rib is a hand transfer. A ribber fabric, racking, half pitch, or a second bed needs the TR-160.

## The ribber

Half pitch puts the ribber needles in the gaps between the main-bed needles. That arrangement is 1×1 rib. Racking is how far the ribber bed slides sideways, in whole needles. The number in the racking indicator is part of that fabric's setup. The comb and the weights are hung before the first pass.

A rib hem is transferred from the ribber onto the main bed, and the main bed then knits stockinette. The LK150 has no ribber for that transfer.

The TR-160/230 manual has steps for 1×1 rib, 2×2 rib, full needle rib, 5×5 rib, circular knitting, English rib, and racking patterns. Fisherman rib is a name Knit It Now uses for the TR-160, and that name is not in the manual text.

## Gauge, yarn, and a body block

Cast-on stitches are the rounded value of stitches per 10 cm times width in cm, divided by 10. Rows are the same formula with rows per 10 cm and length. Inches and centimeters convert by 2.54, rounded to a tenth.

A yarn is recorded with its ball name, its tension dial, stitches and rows per 10 cm, and weight in grams. It can also carry a color and a one-letter symbol. The gauge taken from a swatch is the gauge to use. The swatch's technique is stockinette, tuck, or slip, and a yarn estimate uses a swatch of the same technique.

Yarn weight for a rectangle, from a matching swatch, is swatch grams times piece stitches times piece rows, divided by stitches per 10 cm times rows per 10 cm. When several yarns share the piece, the grams split by each yarn's share of the stitches.

A chart has a width, a height, a stitch repeat, and a row repeat. The repeat fits inside the chart. Row 1 of a drawn chart sits at the bottom.

The straight body block takes centimeters: hem width, length from hem to shoulder, armhole depth, neck depth, and neck width. It is figured with the machine's pitch and the yarn's confirmed gauge.

- Cast-on from the hem width.
- Length in rows from the length.
- Armhole row: rows for (length minus armhole depth), plus one, with 4 stitches decreased on each side.
- Neck: rows for (length minus neck depth), plus one, and a bind-off whose stitch count comes from the neck width.

Neck depth is less than armhole depth, and armhole depth is less than length.

## Open points

- No sweater-prose TH-160 garment is in the sources.
- Fisherman rib is named by Knit It Now and is not a section of the TR-160/230 manual text.
- Motif is a name on the TH-160, with no pass rules beyond the name.
- Brother KH230 and KR230 wording matches some TH-160 paragraphs. Those books are Brother manuals.
- The saved LK150 instruction book is a scan, so its pages do not yield selectable text. The LK150 positions and levers above follow the pattern writing and the Clearwater fairisle walk-through.
